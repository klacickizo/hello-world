# Digital Product Research — Parenting (Nigerian / African mothers) — 2026-10-03

## How to read this

Market: Nigeria plus the African diaspora, sold on Selar. 7 ideas survived, 5 were rejected.

**Limits of this run.** From this environment I could not open Nairaland, Selar, Quora, Daily Trust, Legit.ng or BellaNaija pages (the network blocks them). Everything below comes from search-result titles, URLs and snippets.

- I have **no verbatim post bodies**, so I quote no mothers. Nairaland thread titles are the posters' own words and are used as the problem statements.
- I have no post or view counts.
- I could not read the competitors' paid files, only their listing summaries. Prices are quoted where a snippet showed one.
- Every "gap" is therefore **inferred, not observed**, and capped at 3 on the gap score. Because of that, nothing reaches "Build now".

**The main finding.** Demand is real, but Selar's parenting shelf is crowded on every obvious topic. Tantrums, discipline, picky eating, screen time, teens, puberty talk and homework each already have several listings, many by Nigerian authors. A new ebook has to win on a narrow angle, not on the topic.

## Top picks

### 1. Without the Cane: What to Say and Do Instead (for Christian mothers) — score 75/100 (Strong)
**Niche:** Parenting, discipline | **Buyer:** Christian Nigerian mothers of children 3–12 who want to stop flogging but are pressured by a husband, grandmother, pastor or teacher quoting "spare the rod" | **Price:** ₦3,500 (diaspora $7)

**The problem:** She doesn't want to beat her child. She doesn't know what to do in the moment of the offence, and the adults around her say the cane is the only thing that works.

**Evidence:**
- Nairaland /family help-seeking titles: "Is It Possible To Raise A Child Without Beating" (thread 1888145), "Is It Possible To Discipline A Child Without Flogging Him Or Her?" (709777), "Do You Consider Flogging An Effective Way Of Discipline? What's The Alternative?" (5605966).
- Recurrence: related threads run from low IDs (54071, 71675) to high ones (7047022, 7169290). That proves the question has been asked repeatedly over years.
- Afrobarometer, as returned by search: 55% of Nigerians say physical force is sometimes or always justified, and opposition rose from 28% to 45% since 2017. Many mothers are mid-transition and surrounded by people who are not.

**What they pay for it now:** Selar discipline PDFs at ₦3,000 (Break the Cycle) or $3–5 (Positive Discipline Methods, Calm Mother Reset). A Lagos clinical psychologist costs ₦18,000–51,750 per session.

**What exists:** Break the Cycle, Positive Discipline Methods, Say It Once (a four-move Nigerian system), a "How To Discipline Your Child Without Beating" listing, and **Discipline Without Damage**, framed for Nigerian Muslim homes with 50 word-for-word scripts.

**The gap (unverified):** A Christian-framed guide that answers the "spare the rod" argument head-on and gives scripts for the husband, grandmother or teacher who says "bring the cane". The Muslim edition shows the faith-matched, scripts-first format exists. I found no Christian equivalent in one search, which is weak evidence of absence.

**Contents sketch:**
1. Why you're tired of the cane, and why that doesn't make you a weak parent
2. What the "rod" verses say, and the reading that fits gentle correction (one tradition, chosen deliberately)
3. The moment of the offence: what to say in the first 10 seconds
4. Scripts by offence: lying, rudeness, ignoring you, sibling fights, school-night refusal
5. Consequences that work in a small Nigerian home
6. What to say to the husband, grandmother, pastor or teacher
7. When you've already shouted or beaten: repairing it
8. A 14-day first-reset plan and a one-page printable

**Hook:** You don't want to beat your child. You also don't know what to do instead. Here is exactly what to say.

**Channel:** Facebook parenting and church-women groups, then WhatsApp, then the Selar link. TikTok clips work too, one script each.

**Watch out for:** It must not read as endorsing physical punishment. Don't claim developmental or psychological outcomes. Add a safety pointer for readers whose child is being hurt. Commit to one faith tradition on purpose. **Verify the Christian gap on Selar before writing a word.**

### 2. Feeding Fights, Without the Syrup — score 70/100 (Strong)
**Niche:** Parenting, feeding | **Buyer:** Mothers of toddlers (1–5) who refuse food or eat only swallow and snacks, and who are being told to give an appetite syrup | **Price:** ₦3,000 (diaspora $6)

**The problem:** Mealtime is a daily battle, she fears the child isn't growing, and other mums recommend Apetamin-type syrups.

**Evidence:**
- Nairaland: "All My Kids Are Picky Eaters, Its Driving Me Crazy" (6141725) and "My Baby Has Refused To Eat.please Help" (2894340).
- Six or more Quora questions, and a viral Nigerian mum story on Legit.ng.
- Search results show mothers recommending Apetamin for children.
- A Dubawa investigation says Apetamin is sold openly in Nigerian pharmacies despite bans elsewhere. Its active ingredient is cyproheptadine, an antihistamine.

**What they pay for it now:** Apetamin 200ml at ₦3,870–₦10,900 on GoMed, plus Selar feeding guides.

**What exists:** The 21 Days Picky Eater Reset (includes a "Nigerian Snack Audit"), From Picky Eater to Happy Eater (African families), Help! My Toddler Won't Eat, What Will My Baby Eat (40 Nigerian recipes), Tiny Tummies. That is crowded, so the gap score is only 2.

**The gap (unverified):** None of the listing summaries I saw mention appetite syrups. I couldn't read the paid files, so this is a hypothesis.

**Contents sketch:** why toddlers refuse food; the snack, juice and noodle trap; a mealtime routine that ends the battle; getting swallow-lovers to try other foods; what appetite syrups are and what fact-checkers say; when to see a paediatrician.

**Watch out for:** This one is medical-adjacent. No weight or growth promises, no calorie targets, no endorsing any syrup or supplement, and a visible "see a doctor" line. Debunking the syrup is legitimate. Recommending anything is not.

### 3. The Talk, Without the Shame — score 63/100 (Maybe)
Puberty and sex talk for parents of 8–14 year olds. Problem evidence is real (Nairaland 2098999, 3184847, 5261179), but at least four Selar products already serve it (Beyond the Silence, How To Talk To Your Children About Sex Without Shame, and others), and I found no angle that is not already covered. It is also a child-safety topic that needs careful, non-explicit wording. **Park** it unless a narrower angle shows its own demand.

## Looked at and rejected

- **Postpartum depression / omugwo survival guide.** It's clinical territory, so the guide would need mental-health claims, and four Selar listings already exist.
- **Baby sleep training.** I searched and found only generic Quora questions, with no Nigerian demand signal.
- **Bedwetting and potty training.** Nairaland threads exist (253642, 1384059, 5559618), but bedwetting is health-adjacent and would need medical claims.
- **Working-mum guilt and nanny management.** The evidence is academic studies and a news story, not first-person help-seeking, and four Selar listings exist (Surviving Omugwo & PPD, The Domestic Guide, and others).
- **Single mothers and school fees.** This is a money problem more than a parenting one, so it belongs in the money niche. My search returned only academic papers.

## Market notes

- Selar prices I saw were mostly $3–$7 or ₦3,000 (Break the Cycle, a 30-page PDF). That is the competitor price band. A ₦3,500 domestic price sits just above it, so the book has to justify it with scripts and specifics.
- Dual pricing is the easy win. $7 is about ₦9,300 at ₦1,330/$, and a lot of the shelf sells in USD.
- Whether the Selar listings actually sell is unknown. This market shows few reviews, so I treat price as the signal, not ratings.
- Kaspersky and Afrobarometer figures came through search snippets. Re-check them before putting a number in sales copy.
